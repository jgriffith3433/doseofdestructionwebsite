The Encore: the Foundry furnace floor, the night the Orchestrator steps onto his own show.

Where it is. Every night at midnight the furnace floor of the Foundry becomes a TV stage and the whole city tunes in. This is the home studio of Dose of Destruction, the last stop on the tour (Open Mic to Encore). Every other chapter happened somewhere out on the tour; this one is the set itself. The man who runs the show, the Orchestrator, comes down from the control booth and fights on it. When it's over, his baton hits the furnace floor and the broadcast goes quiet.

The look. A huge old iron foundry turned into a broadcast set. Black iron, riveted girders, chains, ladles and crucibles hung from the roof, and the orange-white glow of the furnace mouths behind everything. Over the industrial bones, the show's dressing: a stage floor of steel plates with a painted show logo ring in the center (no readable text), rows of stage lights on trusses, TV cameras on cranes, a red ON AIR lamp, a conductor's podium (empty, music stand and all) at the back, a crowd in silhouette in the dark galleries. Molten-metal orange and furnace red against cold blue stage light and deep black. Midnight. Smoke and sparks. Grand, theatrical, a finale.

The stage. The main floor is the furnace floor: one wide steel deck over the pour channels, solid, with clean ledges. Below and off the sides, glowing pour channels of molten metal (light only; falling in is just a fall). Above the floor, platforms that belong to the place: a lighting truss catwalk, a crane ladle's rim or a hanging steel plate on chains, the conductor's riser. Keep it fair for a boss fight against a giant fighter: room to move, room to recover, nowhere to camp.

What happens. It's a live show, so the stage reacts to the broadcast:
- the crowd in the galleries cheers and the stage lights flash on every KO;
- now and then the furnace pours: a warning glow, then the pour channels flare and sparks rise (embers), a short damage zone off the edges or below, never over the main floor;
- a hanging ladle or plate may swing slowly.
Fireworks of sparks at the end of the match: a finale.

Rules. No readable text or logos on the art. Nobody standing on the stage: the crowd is silhouettes far in the galleries. The Orchestrator himself is not in the art (he's the fighter). Nothing corporate, no labs, no vats.
