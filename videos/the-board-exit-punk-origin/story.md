# The Board: story (v2)

**Logline.** A kid who runs parts for the Foundry turns up eleven seconds late with the bolt that keeps the furnace alive and a snapped skateboard; the one man who looks after him fixes the board, knowing the kid only rides where nobody can stop him, and when the kid says he wants a way out, gives him the stage upstairs and a single rule so that he comes back in one piece.

**Exit Punk (kid, parts runner).** Wants: to be somewhere that has an exit. Feels about the Mechanic: he'd never say it, but he's the only person who notices him. Attitude: dry, defensive, jokes to cover that he cares.

**The Mechanic.** Wants: the furnace to stay lit and the kid to stay alive, in that order out loud and the other way round in truth. Feels about the kid: tired, protective, third time this month and he'll do it a fourth. Attitude: flat, never looks up, shows love by fixing things.

**The turn.** The Mechanic asks why the kid keeps breaking the board. The kid's answer ("nowhere I'm allowed to ride has an exit") is the first honest thing either says. The Mechanic stops working, tells him about the stage window, and lets him go anyway.

**The payoff.** The rule ("wheels down") is how a man who can't follow says "come back": the kid takes the board and the promise (bring the bolt back), skates to the stage door, the lights come up, and he drops in wheels down.

**Why it matters.** Origin of Exit Punk, the walk-on who is only here for the exit, and of the series callback "wheels down". Candidate canon, not yet confirmed.

**Who it's for.** Anyone: a kid, a gruff mentor, a repair, a goodbye. The bolt and the board are visible, the rule is spoken, the ride is the answer to "an exit".

## One look
Both characters are drawn in the same lit 3D-cartoon render, from the same plate (the Mechanic's corner of the Foundry: the geared machine, steel crate, workbench, orange furnace glow). Every keyframe is an edit of that plate, never a separate portrait on a new background. Screen direction: the Mechanic sits screen-left looking right; Exit Punk sits screen-right looking left. The 180-degree line runs between them; OTS shots stay on their own side.
